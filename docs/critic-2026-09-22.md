## 1. Headline and verdict

**The best-paced version yet. A briefing before every lead finally lets the puzzles breathe, but a handful of old continuity cracks and one new sourcing hole keep it from the top shelf.**

*What Maya Knew* is a father going through his missing daughter's laptop, notebook and corkboard. It now reads like a game that trusts you. Every lead opens on a quiet card: one line from Thomas, two numbered facts with where each came from, one sentence saying what you're about to do, and a big Start button. Then the puzzle gets the whole screen. On a 1440×950 desktop no briefing asks you to read more than 78 words. The puzzles are still the reason to play. The EXIF timeline that clears Corey Marsh, the WHOIS diff that surfaces a name, and the 7:47 clock set against the printed programme all hold up next to *Golden Idol*. Clue cards say plainly where each fact came from. The prologue is spare and human. What still lets it down is the kind of thing a sharp player notices and then stops trusting: a website that was "created" on two different dates, a sticky note that's on the front of the board and then "on the back", a map you're told is labelled when it isn't, and one lead (A9) that hands you three facts as "what you already have" when you may never have seen them. None of these needs a puzzle changed. All of them need fixing before this goes out for review.

### Major

**M1. A9 hands you three facts as "what you already have" that you may never have seen. VERIFIED**
- Where: A9 work screen (desktop 072), gameData.js:474-481. Unlock chain from gameData.js: A6 → A7, A9. A7 → A8. A4 unlocks nothing.
- What: the right panel is headed "STILLWATER_M — WHAT YOU ALREADY HAVE / From the forum, the archive and the registration". It lists:
  - "Privacy shield switched on 9 November" — first shown in A7 (gameData.js:371), a *sibling* of A9, not a prerequisite.
  - "His own photograph of the side gallery… his kit on a folding table" — Flickr, A4, also not a prerequisite.
  - "Ninety-one of his hundred and four posts went up between 11pm and 2am" (gameData.js:480) — this appears nowhere else in the game.
  
  A9 also has no brief.
- Why it matters: this is exactly the "used a fact before it was shown" failure the player called out. The required n-created ↔ s-shield match rests on the 9 November fact.
- Fix (presentation only):
  1. Give each right-hand row its own source line, as the briefings do: "from the WHOIS lookup", "from his Flickr", "from Maya's forum log".
  2. Retitle the panel "STILLWATER_M — WHAT MAYA HAD ON HIM" so it no longer claims the *player* has seen it.
  3. Add an A9 brief: `[{ fact: 'He hid his website\'s owner on 9 November.', from: 'the WHOIS record, on Maya\'s laptop' }, { fact: 'Maya counted his forum posts: 91 of 104 went up between 11pm and 2am.', from: 'Maya\'s forum log' }]`.

**M2. C3 asks for a name "on Maya's corkboard map". The map has no names on it. VERIFIED**
- Where: gameData.js:1128-1131 (prompt, contextNote, wrongFeedback); leadMeta.js:148 hint; CorkPlate.jsx:153-170 (cp-04 draws a street grid, a river and "MILLHAVEN", with pins at :236-239). Desktop 138/140 confirm it by eye.

- Why: a player who does exactly what the game says is stuck, then pays for a hint that repeats the false instruction.
- Fix (art/wording): add handwritten pin labels to cp-04 ("Alder Hall", "PO Box 441", "Courthouse"), matching C8's map_section.txt, and make cp-04's C1 hotspot text say so: "A map of Millhaven.

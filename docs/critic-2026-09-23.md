## 1. Headline and verdict

**A careful, fair and moving OSINT mystery that now plays straight with its twist. What still holds it back is the last layer of writing, not the design.**

*What Maya Knew* puts you in the shoes of a retired reporter going through his missing daughter's laptop, her half-burned notebook and her corkboard, one record at a time. The puzzles are the real thing: you read EXIF times against a time zone, compare two WHOIS snapshots, check a forum poster's "insider" facts against a police appeal, and put newspaper cuttings next to court filings. Three threads of evidence slowly land on one man. It is closer to *The Case of the Golden Idol* than to *Her Story*: structured, fair and generous with its sources. This round removed the last interface tells about the culprit, and the big name reveal now arrives as a full-screen beat. What's left are writing seams. The reveal card prints a full name and a dead wife's first name that the player has never been shown. The menu tells you your words to one named friend "matter". British words sit in an Oregon town. And several screens still lead with 75–80 words of briefing. None of that stops you playing. All of it stops the game feeling finished.

### Major

**M1. The name reveal shows a name and a person the player has never seen.** VERIFIED
- Where: NameRevealCard.jsx:19-24; desktop 187. It is triggered by any of `whois` (A12), `html_author` (B8) or `registry` (C5), caseData.js:578.
- What: the card always types "Raymond T. Callahan". If the name first comes from A12 the record says "R. Callahan", and from B8 it says "Ray Callahan". The middle initial and "Raymond" arrive unsourced at the single most important moment. Line 1 names "Carmen", who appears nowhere earlier in the game (grep: only here and CaseBoard.jsx:769). Line 2, "his name is on the paperwork behind the man who took her", is hard to parse and asserts an abductor the player hasn't shown.
- Why it matters: this breaks requirement 2 exactly where the player is paying most attention. A player who came in through A12 will ask "where did 'Raymond T.' come from?"
- Fix (presentation and wording only): type the name exactly as the record that fired it shows it, and label the record. Pass the triggering clue in and pick from a table: `whois` gives "R. Callahan" with the eyebrow "The September registration, before he hid it"; `html_author` gives "Ray Callahan" with "In the code of his website"; `registry` gives "Raymond T. Callahan" with "Oregon business registry". Rewrite the lines:
  - "Ray held my hand the night my wife died. He taught Maya to drive."
  - "And his name is on the account that was watching Lena."
  - "A name on a form isn't proof. I need to know why it's there." (keep)

**M2. The main menu points at Ray before the game starts.** VERIFIED
- Where: OSINTGuide.jsx:121 (How to Play, main menu).
- What: "Mind what you say. Your oldest friend, Ray, will be texting. What you tell him matters."
- Why it matters: it undoes this round's gating. Before the prologue, one named character is singled out as someone whose reactions to your words matter. Mystery readers know what that means.
- Fix: "Answer your texts. People will write to you while you work. Reply the way Thomas would."

**M3. B11's page art contradicts its own words, and Maya's voice changes.** VERIFIED
- Where: B11 desktop 129, mobile 348; gameData.js:935 ("The fire got most of them"); the B11 hint "There's hardly anything left of these pages"; gameData.js:944-960 vs :693.

Lowercase the B11 text: "he has a key to dad's house. dad gave it to him years ago. / he knows the wifi password. / if I email dad, he might see it first." and "he was at dinner last week. I sat across from him and watched him talk to dad about football." Cut "You know what you did. You know I know." to "I think he knows I know."

## 6. What works (do not lose it)

- **The clue card.** "NEW NOTE", one fact, "WHERE IT CAME FROM", one button. It's the clearest clue moment I've seen in a browser mystery.
- **Sourced briefings.** Every fact has a small "from ..." line. A13's timeline now cites a file on every row. This is the house style. Extend it to the name reveal.
- **Ray's texts.** Warm, specific and a little too helpful. "I could drive up tonight and ask at that coffee place she goes to on Tuesdays" is the best single line in the game, because it is a clue disguised as kindness.
- **Maya's NOTES file.** Lowercase, dated, "none of that was public. I checked. twice." It sounds like a real 24-year-old.
- **Source skins.** Reddit, Wayback, the WHOIS terminal and the scan output look like what they are, so the player learns real OSINT surfaces.
- **A13 and A12.** A UTC offset and a two-snapshot diff are real techniques, fairly clued, and they produce genuine "oh" moments.
- **The gated Ray chip.** The countdown now appears only when a record makes him a person. Keep it that way, and fix the menu line and the monogram so the gating is total.
- **The endings as case files.** Stamp, time bar, "what happened", an Okafor quote. Each reflects the call you made.
- **Mobile robustness.** Clean on 28 leads x 3 viewports at rest and after interaction, and a harness (mobplay, built) that keeps it that way.
- **The prologue's restraint.** Two lines per beat, every fact on a phone screen, no narrator poses.
